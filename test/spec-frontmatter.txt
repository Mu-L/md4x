
# Frontmatter

MD4X always recognizes YAML-style frontmatter blocks delimited by `---` at the
very beginning of the document; the extension is unconditional.

The frontmatter block must start with a line of three or more dashes as the
very first line of the document.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
title: Hello
date: 2024-01-01
---
.
````````````````````````````````

The content after the closing fence is parsed normally.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
title: Hello
---
Hello, world!
.
<p>Hello, world!</p>
````````````````````````````````

A body with nothing at all between the fences is not frontmatter -- see the
`# Empty Frontmatter` section below for exactly where that line falls. A body of
one space is, and yields an empty frontmatter object.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
 
---
.
````````````````````````````````

More than three dashes can be used.

```````````````````````````````` example
-----
title: Hello
-----
.
````````````````````````````````

Trailing spaces after the dashes are allowed, on both the opening and the
closing fence. (Each fence below carries three trailing spaces.)

```````````````````````````````` example
---   
title: Hello
---   
.
````````````````````````````````

Frontmatter must be on the very first line. A leading blank line disables it.

```````````````````````````````` example

---
title: Hello
---
.
<hr>
<h2>title: Hello</h2>
````````````````````````````````

Frontmatter recognition is unconditional and there is no option to turn it
off, so this suite cannot observe the disabled behaviour
(`---` lines parsed as thematic breaks). An example claiming to cover it
used to sit here; its input was byte-identical to the flag-on example above
and its expected output was the flag-on output, so it could only ever pass
for the wrong reason. It was removed rather than reworded.

If the frontmatter is never closed it is not frontmatter: the opening `---` is
an ordinary thematic break and the rest of the document is ordinary markdown.
The `# An Unterminated Opening Fence Is Not Frontmatter` section below covers
the rule in full.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
title: Hello
date: 2024-01-01
.
<hr>
<p>title: Hello
date: 2024-01-01</p>
````````````````````````````````

The closing fence must not have any non-space content after the dashes.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
title: Hello
--- not a closing fence
---
.
````````````````````````````````

Frontmatter cannot appear after other content.

```````````````````````````````` example
Hello
---
title: value
---
.
<h2>Hello</h2>
<h2>title: value</h2>
````````````````````````````````

Up to three spaces of indentation are allowed on the opening fence.

```````````````````````````````` example
   ---
title: Hello
---
.
````````````````````````````````

Up to three spaces of indentation are allowed on the closing fence.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
title: Hello
   ---
.
````````````````````````````````

Four or more spaces of indentation disables frontmatter.

```````````````````````````````` example
    ---
title: Hello
---
.
<pre><code>---
</code></pre>
<h2>title: Hello</h2>
````````````````````````````````

Frontmatter cannot appear inside a container block.

```````````````````````````````` example
> ---
> title: Hello
> ---
.
<blockquote>
<hr>
<h2>title: Hello</h2>
</blockquote>
````````````````````````````````

# Malformed Frontmatter in JSON Output

libyaml reports a syntax error only after it has already emitted the events
preceding it, and the JSON writer streams straight through `process_output` —
so a mid-mapping error cannot be rolled back, only repaired forward. The
contract is that the emitted JSON is always balanced: the pairs libyaml did
parse are kept, every container that was opened is closed, and a key whose
value could not be parsed gets an explicit `null` rather than being dropped
(dropping it would make a truncated document look like one where the author
simply omitted the field). Before the repair these all produced output that
`JSON.parse` rejected.

## Unparseable scalar value

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: @bad
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"a":null},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Pairs parsed before the error are kept

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: Hello
b: @bad
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello","b":null},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Unterminated flow sequence is closed

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: [1
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"a":[1]},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Unterminated flow mapping is closed

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: {x: 1
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"a":{"x":1}},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Sequence element that is not a value

An alias is never resolved (libyaml's parser does not compose), and any other
stray event in a sequence used to leave the separating comma dangling.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: [1, *x]
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"a":[1,null]},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Error inside a nested mapping

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a:
  b: @bad
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"a":{"b":null}},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Unterminated quoted scalar

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: "x
b: [
---
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"a":null},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

# Fence Whitespace

The opening and closing fences tolerate trailing *spaces*, but a trailing tab
is not equivalent: it disables the opening fence while still being accepted on
the closing fence. Both halves of that asymmetry are pinned here so neither can
drift silently.

## Trailing spaces after the dashes keep the block a frontmatter block

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---   
title: Hello
---   
.
{"nodes":[],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello"},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A tab after the opening dashes disables frontmatter

The opening fence scanner accepts only spaces after the dashes, so `---<TAB>`
is an ordinary thematic break and the YAML below it is read as markdown.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---→
title: Hello
---
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["h2",{"id":"title-hello"},"title: Hello"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":2,"text":"title: Hello","id":"title-hello"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A tab after the closing dashes disables frontmatter too

The closing fence scanner accepts only spaces as well, so `---<TAB>` closes
nothing -- and with no closing fence anywhere the opening `---` is not a
frontmatter opener either. Both fences therefore treat a tab the same way, and
the space case above is the only whitespace that keeps the block.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: Hello
---→
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["h2",{"id":"title-hello"},"title: Hello"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":2,"text":"title: Hello","id":"title-hello"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

# Empty Frontmatter

Where an *empty* body stops being frontmatter is a byte, not a line: a block with
no characters at all between its two fences is two thematic breaks, and one
character -- even a single space -- makes it a frontmatter block again, with
`frontmatter` an empty object and nothing contributed to `nodes`. That is where
Comark draws the line (`comark@0.6.2`, probed case by case), and the first half
of it is CommonMark example 98 as well.

The blank-line pair below looks arbitrary and is not: `---` / blank / `---` has
one body line and that line is empty, so there is nothing at all between the
fences, while `---` / blank / blank / `---` has two, and the newline dividing
them is itself a character.

## Nothing between the fences is two thematic breaks

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
---
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["hr",{}]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## The same, followed by content

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
---

# Hi
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["hr",{}],["h1",{"id":"hi"},"Hi"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":1,"text":"Hi","id":"hi"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A body of one blank line is also nothing

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---

---

x
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["hr",{}],["p",{},"x"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A body of two blank lines is frontmatter

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---


---

x
.
{"nodes":[["p",{},"x"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A comment-only body is frontmatter

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
# only a comment
---

x
.
{"nodes":[["p",{},"x"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Inside a component, an empty props block is consumed either way

The two positions part company here, and both follow Comark: a component's props
block has no document content to protect, so an empty `---` block between the
opener and the body is consumed rather than rendered. `spec-components.txt` pins
the rest of that position.

```````````````````````````````` example
::card
---
---
Body
::
.
<card>
<p>Body</p>
</card>
````````````````````````````````

# Parse Result

## Frontmatter and nodes are reported side by side

The worked example from the Comark frontmatter page: a well-formed block must
populate `frontmatter` *and* leave the body in `nodes`. Every other populated
`frontmatter` object in this file comes from a malformed-YAML repair case with
an empty `nodes`, so this is the one example pinning the ordinary shape.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: My Article
author: Jane Doe
---

# Hello World
.
{"nodes":[["h1",{"id":"hello-world"},"Hello World"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"My Article","author":"Jane Doe"},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":1,"text":"Hello World","id":"hello-world"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## YAML scalar types at the JSON boundary

Integers stay numbers; a YAML timestamp has no JSON counterpart and becomes a
string. `depth` and `searchDepth` are ordinary keys with no effect on
`meta.headings` -- all four heading levels below are reported regardless.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
date: 2024-01-15
depth: 3
searchDepth: 2
tags:
  - markdown
  - documentation
custom_field: custom value
---

# Document Content
.
{"nodes":[["h1",{"id":"document-content"},"Document Content"]],"frontmatter":{"date":"2024-01-15","depth":3,"searchDepth":2,"tags":["markdown","documentation"],"custom_field":"custom value"},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":1,"text":"Document Content","id":"document-content"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## The frontmatter body is YAML, never markdown

The `#` and `-` and `**` below stay inside YAML scalars: no heading is created
and `meta.headings` stays empty.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: "# Not a heading"
body: |
  ## also not
  - list
  **bold**
---

real
.
{"nodes":[["p",{},"real"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"# Not a heading","body":"## also not\n- list\n**bold**\n"},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Duplicate YAML keys are emitted twice

The JSON writer streams libyaml's events straight through and does not keep a
seen-key set, so a duplicate YAML key produces a duplicate JSON key. The output
is still balanced and `JSON.parse`-able, and last-wins agrees with YAML's own
resolution -- but a strict JSON consumer may reject it. See the note in the
JSON-repair section below for why the writer cannot look back.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: 1
a: 2
---
x
.
{"nodes":[["p",{},"x"]],"frontmatter":{"a":1,"a":2},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

# Data Binding

## Frontmatter dot-paths in bind props pass through verbatim

The parser does not resolve `:prop="frontmatter.path"`. It keeps the `:` prefix
on the key, carries the path through as a plain string, and exposes the
frontmatter object beside it; resolution is the renderer's job.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
theAnswer: 42
user:
  name: Ada
---

::question{:answer="frontmatter.theAnswer"}
::

Hello, :badge{:label="frontmatter.user.name"}
.
{"nodes":[["question",{":answer":"frontmatter.theAnswer"}],["p",{},"Hello, ",["badge",{":label":"frontmatter.user.name"}]]],"frontmatter":{"theAnswer":42,"user":{"name":"Ada"}},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

# Frontmatter and the Blocks That Follow It

## Frontmatter immediately followed by a block component

No blank line is needed after the closing fence. The `::alert` line must not be
swallowed into the YAML, and must not be mistaken for a component props fence.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: 1
---
::alert{type="info"}
Hi
::
.
{"nodes":[["alert",{"type":"info"},["p",{},"Hi"]]],"frontmatter":{"a":1},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A setext heading directly after the closing fence

The closing fence is consumed by the frontmatter, so `Subtitle` / `---` is an
ordinary setext h2 rather than a second fence.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: Hello
---
Subtitle
---
.
{"nodes":[["h2",{"id":"subtitle"},"Subtitle"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello"},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":2,"text":"Subtitle","id":"subtitle"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A second `---` block after the closing fence is plain markdown

There is exactly one frontmatter block per document; the parser never re-enters
the frontmatter state.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
a: 1
---
---
b: 2
---

x
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["h2",{"id":"b-2"},"b: 2"],["p",{},"x"]],"frontmatter":{"a":1},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":2,"text":"b: 2","id":"b-2"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

# Line Endings and Byte-Order Marks

Frontmatter opts out of the parser's tab-preservation path, so its line
normalization is bespoke; these examples pin that `\r` never survives into a
YAML scalar and that a BOM does not push the opening fence off line 1. The
inputs below contain literal CR bytes and a literal U+FEFF -- do not "clean up"
this section's whitespace.

## CRLF line endings

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: Hello
count: 42
---

# Hi
.
{"nodes":[["h1",{"id":"hi"},"Hi"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello","count":42},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":1,"text":"Hi","id":"hi"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Lone CR line endings

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: Hello
---
# Hi
.
{"nodes":[["h1",{"id":"hi"},"Hi"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello"},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":1,"text":"Hi","id":"hi"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A UTF-8 BOM before the opening fence

A BOM must not count as leading content: were it to disable frontmatter, the
unterminated-fence rule below would then eat the rest of the file.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
﻿---
title: Hello
---

# Hi
.
{"nodes":[["h1",{"id":"hi"},"Hi"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello"},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":1,"text":"Hi","id":"hi"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

# Frontmatter in the Other Renderers

## Document frontmatter never reaches ANSI output

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: My Article
author: Jane Doe
---

body text
.
body text
.
--format=ansi
````````````````````````````````

## Full-HTML `<head>` escapes frontmatter values

`title` and `description` are the only place a frontmatter value is written
into markup, so neither may break out of its tag or attribute.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title: "a\"><script>x</script>"
description: "d\"><b>"
---

# C
.
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html>
<head>
<title>a&quot;&gt;&lt;script&gt;x&lt;/script&gt;</title>
<meta name="generator" content="md4x">
<meta charset="UTF-8">
<meta name="description" content="d&quot;&gt;&lt;b&gt;">
</head>
<body>
<h1>C</h1>
</body>
</html>
.
--full-html
````````````````````````````````

## A non-scalar `title` degrades to an empty `<title>` -- mapping

No YAML fragment may leak into `<head>`. `--format=json` still reports the
mapping faithfully.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]

title:
  a: 1

.

<meta charset="UTF-8">

</body>
</html>
.
--full-html
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]

  - x
  - y

.
````````````````````````````````

## The same mapping `title` is still reported in full by the JSON renderer

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]

  a: 1

.
````````````````````````````````

# An Opening Fence Only Opens Frontmatter When YAML Follows It

Frontmatter is *enclosed* by `---` delimiters (Comark `frontmatter.md`) and
carries a YAML mapping, so an opening fence fails to open a frontmatter block on
two counts: when no closing fence follows it anywhere, and when the body between
the fences reads as markdown rather than as YAML. Either way the `---` is an
ordinary thematic break and everything after it is ordinary markdown, exactly as
the `***` and `___` spellings behave. Whether a document survives therefore no
longer depends on which of the three synonymous thematic break spellings its
author happened to type.

The classifier settles both at the opening fence, looking ahead once for a
closing fence and once at the first line of the body. The body test skips blank
lines and `#` comments and then requires a mapping key -- `key:` followed by a
space or the line end. An *empty* body (nothing but blanks and comments before
the closing fence) still opens frontmatter: it has no content to lose, and
Comark resolves it to an empty object rather than to markdown. See the `# Empty
Frontmatter` section above, and `spec-comark.txt`.

The two controls at the end of this section (`***` and `___`) are kept: they are
what makes the equivalence visible in one place.

## A body that is a bare paragraph is not frontmatter

"Hello world" used to be discarded here: the second `---` is a closing fence, so
the block was read as frontmatter and only the text below it survived. A
paragraph is not a YAML mapping, so the block is now a thematic break and the
document is preserved in full.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---

Hello world

---

Bye
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["p",{},"Hello world"],["hr",{}],["p",{},"Bye"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## The same input in HTML

```````````````````````````````` example
---

Hello world

---

Bye
.
<hr>
<p>Hello world</p>
<hr>
<p>Bye</p>
````````````````````````````````

## CommonMark example 96: setext headings between thematic breaks

The shape the rule exists for. Before it, the first two lines were eaten as a
YAML scalar and both the `<hr>` and the `<h2>Foo</h2>` vanished.

```````````````````````````````` example
---
Foo
---
Bar
---
Baz
.
<hr>
<h2>Foo</h2>
<h2>Bar</h2>
<p>Baz</p>
````````````````````````````````

## A top-level sequence is not a mapping either

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
- a
- b
---

Content
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["ul",{},["li",{},"a"],["li",{},"b"]],["hr",{}],["p",{},"Content"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## `key:value` with no space after the colon is a scalar, not a mapping

YAML requires the space; without it the line is the plain scalar `title:Hello`,
so the block is markdown -- an `<hr>`, then `title:Hello` under a setext `---`.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
title:Hello
---

Content
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["h2",{"id":"titlehello"},"title:Hello"],["p",{},"Content"]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[{"level":2,"text":"title:Hello","id":"titlehello"}]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A `#` comment above the first key does not disqualify the block

Comments are skipped when looking for the mapping key, so the ordinary habit of
annotating frontmatter keeps working.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
# the document title
title: Hello
---

Content
.
{"nodes":[["p",{},"Content"]],"frontmatter":{"title":"Hello"},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## A quoted key whose text contains `: ` is still a key

The body test scans the quoted scalar before looking for the colon, so a key
that embeds the mapping punctuation does not read as a paragraph.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
"a: b": 1
---

Content
.
{"nodes":[["p",{},"Content"]],"frontmatter":{"a: b":1},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## With no second `---`, nothing is frontmatter and nothing is lost

The document is a thematic break followed by two paragraphs. This is the shape
the rule exists for -- a plain document whose author happened to start it with a
horizontal rule -- and it now renders in full.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
---
Some intro paragraph.

More.
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["p",{},"Some intro paragraph."],["p",{},"More."]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## The same input in HTML

```````````````````````````````` example
---
Some intro paragraph.

More.
.
<hr>
<p>Some intro paragraph.</p>
<p>More.</p>
````````````````````````````````

## Control: the `---` spelling now behaves like the other two

`***` and `___` open no frontmatter, and neither does an unclosed `---`, so the
same document written with any of the three is preserved in full.

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
***

Hello world

More.
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["p",{},"Hello world"],["p",{},"More."]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````

## Control: the same document with `___`

```````````````````````````````` example [no-normalize]
___

Hello world

More.
.
{"nodes":[["hr",{}],["p",{},"Hello world"],["p",{},"More."]],"frontmatter":{},"meta":{"headings":[]}}
.
--format=json
````````````````````````````````
